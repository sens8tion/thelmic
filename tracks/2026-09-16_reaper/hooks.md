## 6. Key and pitch classes

Root is the modal pitch class of the *bass's own* fundamental across the section. The hook histogram is built from the melodic fundamental - one pitch class per frame, weighted by salience - rather than from the full chroma, because a chroma folded from every partial turns to mush in a DJ mix (each note smears onto its own 3rd and 5th, and two records overlap at every transition). Everything is an interval above the root: no absolute notes, no transcription, nothing playable.

## 8. Method and its limits

- Analysis runs on the cached **8 kHz mono** downmix, so nothing above 4 kHz is visible. Hi-hat sparkle, air and the top octave of any bell are outside the measurement; the register conclusions are about fundamentals and lower partials.

- **Harmonic/percussive separation** uses a 0.67 s median along time and a 160 Hz median along frequency. That window smears attacks, so the attack figures in section 4 are a coarse envelope shape, not a true transient time - treat 'sustain share' as ordinal.

- **Type labels are shorthand for measurements**, not instrument identification. From a dense mono mix `vocal-like` means 'pitch glides >=3 semitones with >=8 partials over 0.35-2 s' - a toasted vocal and a portamento lead are not separable here.

- This is a **DJ set**: two records overlap at every transition (grid.npz marks 8 bar-phase resets). Per-section keys and roots are indicative; the aggregate interval shape is the reliable part.

- The detector scores prominence against **each section's own 40th-percentile tonal bed**. A genuinely continuous pad is therefore baseline, not an event. That is deliberate - it matches what the ear does - but it means 'bar occupancy' measures *changing* melodic material, not 'is any pitched sound present'.

- Section boundaries come from novelty on band energies, not from musical analysis; they approximate track and arrangement changes.

## What this means for building a jungle track

Every number below is measured from the reference, not chosen. Bars are 1.446 s at 166.0 BPM.

1. **A hook sounds in 39% of bars - 3.1 bars in every 8 - and this is a dense DJ set, not a sparse one.** 332 of 850 bars carry any pitched melodic or vocal event. Per section the range is 21% to 58%, median 37%. Density is 2.6 events per 8 bars (range 1.3-5.2). **Target ~3 of every 8 bars carrying a hook, at ~3 events per 8 bars.** A 32-bar section with melodic material in more than 18 of its bars is busier than anything in the reference.

2. **Hooks are fragments: 1.2 beats, 0.42 s.** Quartiles 0.8-1.9 beats. **81% of events are under 2 beats**; only 3% reach a full bar. The longest single event in 1260 s is 2.8 s (7.6 beats). Write 1-2 beat motifs - a 4-bar melody line has no precedent here. And they are **struck, not swelled**: median onset-to-peak is 224 ms of that 0.42 s, leaving a sustain of 40%, so use short percussive envelopes - stabs and chops - not long fades.

3. **Leave 1.5 bars of silence between appearances, often more.** Median gap from the end of one hook to the start of the next is 1.54 bars (top-end tier 1.79); 75th percentile 3.3, 90th 5.7, max 23. **41% of gaps exceed 2 bars and 17% exceed 4.** If the melodic lane never goes quiet for 2 bars, it is over-filled.

4. **Repeat sparingly: most shapes never come back at all.** Of 128 distinct hook shapes, only 50 (39%) ever recur; a recurring one returns a median of 3 times across the set (max 20). When a shape does repeat, the interval is a median of 3.6 bars (quartiles 1.4-7.7), and bar-lag self-similarity of the whole melodic layer peaks at 2 bars per section with 4 and 8 next most common (best-lag counts {1: 7, 4: 4, 2: 3, 8: 3}). Consecutive runs are short: a median of 1 back-to-back appearances before the shape changes or leaves (75th pct 2, max 12). **Practical form: state a motif, bring it back 3-ish times on a 4- or 8-bar spacing, then replace it. Nothing in the reference plays every bar for 16 bars.**

5. **Register: fundamental 233-392 Hz, energy spanning 233 Hz to 1568 Hz.** Median fundamental 277 Hz; 91% of events have a fundamental between 196 and 700 Hz and essentially none (0.3%) above 784 Hz. Centre of mass 545 Hz. **There is no separate top-octave hook layer in this set.** Running the detector on 784 Hz+ alone finds 39% bar occupancy - almost exactly the 39% of the full band - because it is firing on the *upper harmonics of the same mid-register events*, not on a distinct high line. So: put the hook's fundamental in roughly 233-392 Hz and let its own harmonics carry it up to ~1568 Hz. Reach for top-end sparkle only as a deliberate exception.

6. **Put the hook's bottom +5 semitones above where the bass stops and keep the notch between them empty.** During an event 90% of the harmonic 25-500 Hz power is below 174 Hz while the hook's lowest 10% starts at 233 Hz; the gap is positive in 62% of events. Between them sits a **10 dB valley at 233 Hz**. Concretely: high-pass the melodic lane near 208 Hz, keep the bass's own content under 174 Hz, and let neither fill 185-282 Hz.

7. **Light-against-dark is register and tonality, not treble.** When a hook sounds its centre of mass is **3.1 octaves above the low band's centre** (545 Hz over 65 Hz), and the harmonic share of the 200 Hz-4 kHz band rises by **+0.113** - a pitched element cutting through a noisy break. Do not chase brightness: the full-mix centroid moves -19 Hz, because the break already owns everything above 2 kHz. Give the hook +1.8 dB on the low band and let register and tonality do the work.

8. **Do not duck the sub for the hook - thin the low-end *pattern* instead.** This one contradicts the obvious advice, so it is worth stating plainly: hook placement is uncorrelated with low-end level (-0.004 against 20-60 Hz, -0.013 against 20-200 Hz), and the low-band centroid barely moves (-0 Hz). The reference does not turn the bass down to make room. What *does* change is low-end rhythmic activity: low-band flux drops **-2.1 dB** while a hook sounds - fewer kick and bass onsets under it. Give the hook a bar where the drum pattern is sparser, not a bar where the sub is quieter.

9. **In key, minor, four notes.** Hook pitch weight lands on **5, b3, 1, 4** relative to the section root - 48% of all hook weight on four pitch classes - and within a single section the top four carry 77%. The minor third outweighs the major 12% to 6%, and 11 of 17 sections read minor. Hook and bass draw on the same pitch set (median agreement 0.50). Use a 4-5 note set in the section's key, not a scale run.

10. **Fragments answering the drums, not a second melody.** 79% of events are stabs or short riff fragments; only 3% are sustained tones or lead lines. Median simultaneous pitch classes during an event is 2 - dyads and small chords, not stacks. A pad, if you want one, should be the continuous bed the fragments are measured against: this detector treats a constant pad as the baseline rather than an event, and so does the ear.
