## Method, and what to distrust

**Tempo.** The cached grid (165.83 BPM, beat confidence 1.04) is not usable - a beat-range autocorrelation lands anywhere in the comb of 16th / 8th / beat harmonics, which are all nearly the same height. Re-derived here from the **four-bar** autocorrelation lag instead, which has no competitor near it: peaks at 2891.5 ms and 5783.0 ms are exactly 2 and 4 bars, giving **165.96 BPM, bar = 1446.1 ms, beat = 361.5 ms**. The tempo is constant across the set (beatmatched).

Independent check that this grid is real: **86% of the drop-to-drop intervals land within 0.35 bar of a whole number of bars**, and 8 of them sit on 8-bar multiples (15.9, 16.0, 24.0, 32.1, 32.1, 48.0, 64.0, 80.1 bars). That does not happen against a wrong tempo.

*Cross-check:* the grid analysis in `grid.md` reaches **165.99 +- 0.02 BPM** (beat 361.459 ms) by a completely different route - least-squares line fits to tracked beat times. This analysis gets 165.96 BPM (beat 361.5 ms) from the four-bar autocorrelation lag. Agreement to 0.03 BPM. That file also finds a **+114 ms (+0.31 beat) phase step** at one mix point with the tempo unchanged, which is why the bar phase here is re-estimated locally per event rather than from one global grid.

**Detection.** Whole-file sweep of a median step function - median of the 4 bars after minus the median of the 4 bars before, with a 0.4 s guard band so the transient belongs to neither side - run on low-band (20-120 Hz) level and on RMS. Four bars, because that is the unit a jungle arrangement moves in; 8 bars smears a drop that is answered 8 bars later, 1 bar fires on every fill. Kept when the low band steps >= 6 dB, or >= 4 dB with >= 3 dB of RMS. Each event is then snapped to the steepest low-band move within +-3 s and, for a drop, to the nearest onset.

**Caveats.** (1) This is a DJ set, so at any mix point two records overlap and some events are the incoming record's, not the outgoing one's - structurally it is still what the listener hears. (2) The 8-bar before/after windows are **clamped at the neighbouring event**; several events sit 8-10 s apart and an unclamped window would average a drop together with the breakdown feeding it and report a delta of nearly zero. The true window length is given per event. (3) 'Call' is a spectral-balance proxy (400-2k energy tilting up against 2-8k, sustained >= 120 ms) - it cannot tell a vocal from an organ chord and does not try. (4) Band levels are dB of summed STFT magnitude, so they are comparable to each other and across time, but they are not absolute SPL.

### What the deltas say

A drop here is **not a lift, it is a re-weighting**. Median across the 22 drops: sub **+9.1 dB**, bass **+3.9 dB** - but mid **-0.3 dB**, high **-0.1 dB**, and the spectral centroid moves **-248 Hz**. Overall RMS only rises **+2.2 dB**. Restricting to the 17 real drops (step >= 8 dB): sub **+10.0 dB** (IQR +3.0 to +20.5), bass **+4.7 dB**, RMS **+2.6 dB**, centroid **-394 Hz**.

- The mid band falls on **55%** of drops and the high band on **50%**. The centroid falls on **64%**.
- Stereo width **narrows** on 64% of drops (median -0.028); crest factor falls on 91% (median -0.26) - the drop is denser and more centred, not just louder.
- Onset density barely moves: median **+0.2 onsets/bar**, and 82% of drops change it by less than 2/bar. **The drums do not get busier at the drop.**

Breakdowns are the mirror image, and they are *not* quiet: sub **-7.8 dB** and low **-4.7 dB**, but mid **+0.3 dB**, centroid **+277 Hz**, crest **+0.31**, and onset density goes **UP** (+0.6/bar, rising on 64% of them). RMS only drops -1.3 dB. Taking the bass out makes the record brighter, busier and spikier - it does not make it quieter.

**The dominant device is subtraction, and it is nearly the only one.** 82% of drops are set up by keeping the break running at full density while the low end is pulled out by a median of **19.2 dB**. Every other device is a minority: rolls 27%, sweeps 23%, calls 18%. **There is not one reverse swell or riser in the set (0%).** This is the user's theory measured: the drop is paid for by the absence in front of it, not by a signpost pointing at it.

The zero is not a threshold artifact. Only **2 of 22 drops have even a 4 dB rise in level** over the 8 bars leading into them, and neither is a riser: 0:49.28 rises +6.7 dB but its low band rises +16 dB with it (the bass returning early, not a riser over an empty low end); 5:15.23 rises +5.0 dB with the low end still 9 dB down, but its crest factor *rises* +0.24 - a drum fill getting louder, not a sustained swell. The median drop is approached by a level that is **falling** (-1.3 dB), not rising.

Median time the low end had been out before a drop: **1.2 bars**. 59% of drops have at least 1 bar of it, but only 23% have 4 bars or more. The absence is usually short.

## 3. The gap

A true hole right before a drop is **the exception, not the rule**.

- **27%** of drops (6 of 22) have >= 80 ms of near-silence (-12 dB) in the last 3 s.
- **9%** (2 of 22) have a true hole at -20 dB.
- When there is a gap at all, the median is **80 ms = 0.22 beats** - a fraction of a beat, a flam, not a bar of silence.
- Non-zero gaps, sorted (ms): 11, 43, 43, 64, 64, 64, 96, 128, 235, 256, 288, 1067. Only one exceeds half a bar.

At 166.0 BPM a beat is 362 ms and a 16th is 90 ms, so the typical gap is roughly **a single 16th of silence**, and the biggest normal one is about a beat. The set never stops dead for a bar to announce a drop.

So the set is in its loud state roughly **6 bars out of every 7**, and the reduced state is typically **1-2 bars**, occasionally 4, 7 or 15. The absence is a short, frequent punctuation, not a long section.

## What this means for building a jungle track

1. **Move the low end, not the fader.** A drop = sub **+10 dB** and bass **+5 dB** (IQR +3 to +20 dB on sub) while mid and high go *down* about **1 dB** and the centroid falls **~400 Hz**. Total RMS should rise only **2-3 dB**. If your drop is +6 dB of everything, it is a volume automation, not a drop.
2. **Spend the bar before on subtraction, not on a signpost.** 82% of drops are set up by the break running at full density with the low end pulled **19 dB** down. Keep the drums at >= 70% of their normal onset rate through the gap - do not thin them out.
3. **Build no risers.** Zero of 22 drops use a reverse swell. Rolls (27%), filter sweeps (23%) and stabs (18%) are each a minority device - use at most one, on about one drop in four, never on all of them.
4. **Keep the gap to one 16th.** Only 27% of drops have any near-silence at all and only 9% a true hole. When you do use one, make it **~80 ms** - one 16th note is 90 ms at this tempo, so that is 0.22 of a beat - not a bar. One gap of about a beat (362 ms) per track is the ceiling; the whole set contains exactly one longer than that.
5. **The absence is short.** Median low-end absence before a drop is **1.2 bars**; only 23% run to 4 bars or more. Reduced passages median **2.1 bars**. A 16-bar breakdown is a once-per-track event, not the pattern.
6. **Drop everything in on bar 1.** No layered entry: bar 2 is within 0.5 dB of bar 1, onset count is flat from bar 1. The only staged element is the sub, which may add **+2.1 dB into bar 2** - i.e. hold or gap the first bass note, then let the line run.
7. **Space drops 33 bars apart.** Median 48 s = 33 bars, with 16, 24, 32, 48 and 64 bars all appearing. Land them on whole bars - 86% of the set's intervals do. Something structural should happen every ~15 bars, but only half of those are drops.
8. **Stay loud 86% of the time.** Roughly 6 bars full to 1 bar reduced. Full runs median 12 bars. Surprise is only legible against a long-held steady state - if you are reducing every 8 bars, the reduction stops meaning anything.
9. **Make the breakdown brighter and busier, not quieter.** Pulling the bass costs only 1.3 dB of RMS while the centroid rises **+277 Hz**, crest rises **+0.31** and onset density goes **up** on 64% of breakdowns. Chop the break harder when the bass leaves.
10. **Narrow the image at the drop.** Stereo width falls on 64% of drops (median -0.028) and crest falls 0.26. The drop is the most mono, most dense moment - keep the wide material for the breakdown.
