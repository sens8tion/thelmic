# Jungle research brief (2026-09-15)

Input to the jungle capability probe on `track/2026-09-15_jungle`: 170 BPM,
F minor, sampled breaks sliced in Simpler, composed by hand over LOM. Gathered
by a research agent from production guides, producer interviews and forum
threads, and paraphrased. Dogs On Acid, Reddit and most Gearspace pages blocked
fetching, so claims tagged **[snippet]** come from search-result summaries only
and are weaker. **[computed]** is arithmetic, **[unsourced]** found no source.
Numbers in brackets index the source list at the bottom.

The reference DJ set supplied for the critique round is for structural
analysis only; nothing from it goes into the track.

## 1. Break chopping

- Two-step skeleton (16 steps, 1 = downbeat): kick 1 and 11, snare 5 and 13,
  50-60% swing at 168-178 BPM [2]. Second kick one 8th before the second snare [5].
- Variations [5]: displaced second snare one 8th either way (step 11 or 15);
  half-time snare on step 9; second kick on step 8.
- Amen feel [6][37]: snares on 2 and 4, a double kick before beat 4 with the
  first hit quieter, a late-bar snare louder than a ghost but softer than the
  backbeat; in bars 3-4 the beat-4 snare lands half a beat late (step 15).
  Dynamics carry the feel. Snare displacement and 16th pickups are the core.
- Shuffle: offbeat closed hat, ghost snare on the next 16th, pickups before
  beat 3 and before the next downbeat [snippet, 37]. A much quieter ghost line
  fills the gaps with varied velocity [3].
- Edit idioms: reorder 16th slices (e.g. 1-3-2-5-4-6-2-1) [14][21]; pitch single
  slices (snare up an octave as a fill or ghost, kick down a few semitones for
  weight) [14]; pitch the last snare of a bar for variation [12]; 4-bar
  A-B-reversed B-snare roll [1]; retrigger and sample-start offsets for rolls
  [17][snippet, 43]; 32nd rolls with a velocity ramp [snippet, 43]; layer a second
  break 1-1.5 beats offset [18] or only for character and top end [snippet, 41].
- Paradox: a "kickback" (single kick + hat) at bar intervals resets the loop;
  small gaps between hat and snare slices give bite [11].
- Change rate: edit-style varies every 4-8 bars [17] with a turnaround every 8
  [25] and a fill closing each 8-bar phrase [2]. Rollers hold, changing at 16/32 [33].
- Programmed vs legit: move slices in quarter/8th blocks to keep the break's
  own micro-timing, rather than hard-quantising every slice [7][14]; accent the
  first downbeat instead of flat kick levels [11]; avoid fast double kicks above
  160 BPM [11]; 1-3 ms fades on slice edges, auto-slicing is ~80% accurate [14].
  Photek built breaks from single hits with varied attack velocity [35].

## 2. Break processing

- Original tempos: Amen ~136 BPM (to 170 = +3.86 st) [14][computed]; Apache and
  Scorpio ~118 (+6.32 st) [snippet, 45][46]; Cold Sweat ~113, sources 112-120
  (+7.07 st) [47][computed]. Our on-disk copies are already at 160/174.
- Repitch practice: small detunes (~+20 cents) to fit near-tempo breaks [16];
  slice at original tempo, remap, then raise BPM [11].
- 90s sampler colour: S950 is 12-bit, "bandwidth" = rate / 2.5; low rates are
  muffled, the grit came from driving the input hot [19][snippet, 50]. E-mu
  rounder and darker, Akai brighter and crunchier [7]. Stretch artefacts are
  metallic and stuttering [22].
- EQ/dynamics: Attack's chain HP 214 Hz, cut 303 Hz, LP 4.84 kHz, layered kick,
  ducking ~8 dB, Glue 4:1 ~-5 dB, 540 ms room send [1]. Elsewhere HP ~100-160 Hz,
  LP ~6 kHz [14][16], room reverb filtered 300 Hz-7 kHz with parallel saturation
  [26], bracketing EQ on layered loops [3].
- Always layer a kick under the break [1][14][20]. Distort then compress to
  restore attack; split kick/snare/hats for separate dynamics [20].

## 3. Bass

- Drums at 160-175, bass written as if at 80-88 BPM: long sliding notes that
  answer the kick rather than doubling it [23]; reggae/rocksteady roots and
  fifths, pentatonic, glides [snippet, 42]. Simple breaks -> busier bass, and
  vice versa [17].
- Sound: pure sine subs [24], or a long 808 [18]. Reese = two detuned saws [24].
  Keep the sine sub clean and separate from any distorted mid layer [28].
- Range: E-G is the sub sweet spot [25][26]; F1 = 43.65 Hz, C2 = 65.4 Hz [computed].
- Mono below 120 Hz; kick focus 80-120 Hz above the sub [27]; HP the kick ~85 Hz
  [2]. Sidechain 1-5 ms attack, 80-150 ms release, 4-6:1 [27]; prefer
  band-limited ducking [28]. Pitch everything up except the main sub [18].

## 4. Harmony and atmosphere

- Darkside: sinister stabs, menacing strings, hoovers [31]. Intelligent/
  atmospheric: melody over complex edits, minor 7ths/9ths [30][snippet, 49].
- Pads: stacked high and low synths, big reverbs, metallic/bell layers [34];
  FM as a supporting edge layer [28]; Sully uses an FM pad and Rhodes through
  big reverb [13].
- Ragga vocals: short chants in drops, call and response, intelligible, on the
  slow grid [snippet, 48][23].
- F-minor progressions: [unsourced].

## 5. Arrangement

- Modern D&B template [15]: intro 32, build 16, drop 64 (2x32), breakdown 32
  (often drumless), build 16, drop 2 64 (harder), outro 32. 32 bars at 170 = ~45 s.
  Phrase on 16/32 boundaries [23].
- Drop signals: accelerating snare roll, a riser that cuts, optionally a beat of
  silence [15]. One-bar roll recipes [4]: 16ths with rising velocity, pitch or
  filter rise, stop at beat 2.4 and leave a gap, quarters -> 8ths -> 16ths;
  velocity steps -12/-9/-6/-3 dB [12].
- Transitions: drop the drums, swap breaks, filter, pull the bass, reverse
  crash [25]. Rewinds are DJ practice, not production [36].

## 6. Mix and FX

- Bass peaks 4-6 dB above kick, kick 3-4 dB above the rest [snippet, 40].
- Mud 150-500 Hz: snare notch ~300 Hz, pad cut ~250 Hz; snare snap 5 kHz, hats
  8 kHz+ [27]. HP pads/returns 100 Hz, hats 200 Hz+ [27]. Kick, snare, sub, lead
  centred [28].
- Dub: automated snare throws, spring reverb, sirens, filtered delay feedback
  [9]. Short early-reflection reverb for depth [snippet, 51]; Paradox keeps the
  break mono and centred [11]; Nookie's Cloud 9 mix was dry [29].

## 7. Top mistakes

1. Grid-locked slices [7][14]. 2. Flat dynamics [6][11]. 3. Clicks from
auto-slicing [14]. 4. Busy break plus busy bass [17]. 5. Random break stacking
[3][snippet, 41]. 6. Thin break without a layered kick/sub [14][20]. 7. Phase
problems in mono [7]. 8. Over-compression [snippet, 41]. 9. No change, or random
change every bar [17][33]. 10. Fast double kicks at jungle tempo [11].
11. Accidental chipmunk pitch [14].
