## (a) Core claims, with evidence quality

**A1. Surprise is measurable per event as −log₂ p(event | context), and listeners' expectedness ratings track it.** IDyOM (Pearce 2005/2012) computes, for every note, information content IC = −log₂ p and entropy H = −Σ p·log₂ p of the predictive distribution, from a variable-order Markov (PPM*) model mixing a long-term model (corpus) and a short-term model (the piece so far). Ratings, reaction times and neural responses correlate with IC across many studies. **Strong.**

**A2. Liking is an inverted-U in surprise and in uncertainty, and the two interact.** Gold, Pearce et al. (2019): across 55 melodies liking showed significant negative quadratic effects of mean IC and of mean entropy; the IC model alone explained 26% (Study 1) and 42% (Study 2) of liking variance; when IC was low, listeners preferred *higher*-entropy stimuli. Cheung, Harrison, Pearce, Koelsch et al. (2019): 1,039 pop chords; pleasure was predicted by uncertainty × surprise: an unsurprising chord in a high-uncertainty context, or a surprising chord in a low-uncertainty context, is pleasant. **Strong** (two independent designs, behavioural + fMRI). This is the empirical spine of the lead's theory: dwelling in "understood" (low IC, low H) or in "no pattern" (high IC, high H) is disliked; pleasure lives in transitions.

**A3. Tonal hierarchy is stable and gives a zero-order surprise measure.** Krumhansl & Kessler (1982) probe-tone profiles; Krumhansl–Schmuckler key-finding correlates a pitch-class duration histogram with the 24 transposed profiles. **Strong** for Western listeners; the hierarchy is flatter in rock (Music Perception 2021). Lerdahl & Krumhansl (2007) tonal-tension model: the hierarchical component predicts tension ratings; the melodic "attraction" component is **not** empirically supported (**moderate/contested**). GTTM (Lerdahl & Jackendoff 1983) supplies grouping/metric preference rules but has weak direct quantitative validation as a whole (**moderate**).

**A4. Expectation-based pleasure has an anticipatory component distinct from the peak.** Huron (2006) ITPRA: the "prediction response" rewards correct prediction and is misattributed to the stimulus; Salimpoor et al. (2011): dopamine in caudate during anticipation, nucleus accumbens at peak. **Strong** for the dissociation; the ITPRA sub-mechanisms themselves are theory, **moderate**.

**A5. Harmonic surprise in hit songs is higher on average and *more variable across sections*.** Miles et al. (2017, 2021): S(chord) = −log₂ P(chord | key-normalised baseline corpus); top-quartile Billboard songs had higher absolute surprise and higher "contrastive surprise" (SD of surprise across sections); verses, not choruses, carried most of the difference. **Moderate** (corpus correlational; a 2022 behavioural follow-up supports it).

**A6. Long-term recognisability favours conventional, repeated, vocal-prominent material — not surprising material.** Hooked on Music (Burgoyne et al. 2013; Van Balen et al. 2015): mean recognition ≈5 s, fastest ≈2.3 s; recognisability predicted by vocal prominence (largest effect), corpus conventionality of timbre/melody/harmony, within-song timbral recurrence, and *lower* melodic entropy. **Strong** (N > 12,000; but marginal R² only ≈0.10 — most variance is song-level).

**A7. Earworms are predicted by a common global contour, an unusual contour gradient, and faster tempo.** Jakubowski et al. (2017): 3-feature random forest, cross-validated accuracy 62.5%; INMI tunes 124 vs 116 bpm. **Moderate** (accuracy only modestly above chance).

**A8. Repetition itself is musicalising and pleasurable; a few exposures suffice.** Deutsch (2011): ten verbatim repetitions turn speech into song; transposition or syllable reordering abolishes it. Margulis (2013): inserting repetitions into Berio/Carter raised enjoyment, interest and artistry ratings. Peretz et al. (1998): a single prior exposure raises both recognition and liking of unfamiliar melodies. Schellenberg et al. (2008): with focused listening, liking peaks after 2 exposures and falls by 8–32; with incidental listening it rises linearly. **Strong.**

**A9. With repeated hearing, attention shifts to longer spans.** Margulis (2012): on early hearings listeners detect ~1 s repetitions; only after many hearings do they detect long (up to 8 s) repetitions, and short-unit detection then declines; detection optimal around 6 s units. **Moderate** (single study, but central to modelling "return").

**A10. Return is experienced as reward, not boredom, when it is predicted and when the preceding section was uncertain.** Direct evidence is thin: an fMRI study of familiar sonata-form pieces finds theme recurrence is anticipated via auditory imagery and this anticipation appears rewarded; chills cluster at new/unprepared harmonies, sudden textural change and the *entry of a voice* (Sloboda 1991; Grewe et al. 2007); Cheung's "unsurprising event in high-uncertainty context is pleasant" quadrant is exactly the reprise; Miles's contrastive-surprise finding is the same at section scale. **Moderate** (converging but indirect).

**A11. Auditory streams form on onset asynchrony, spectral distance, harmonicity, common fate, timbre and location; the perceptual transition is sharp.** Bregman (1990). Rasch (1978): 10 ms onset asynchrony increases separateness, 30 ms makes tones "clearly distinct"; detection of a note in a chord improves from about 0/−20 dB (synchronous) to −60 dB (asynchronous). Onset asynchronies >20–40 ms increase stream segregation. Van Noorden (1975): fission boundary ≈ a few semitones, rate-independent; temporal-coherence boundary strongly rate-dependent (integration range 4 → 13 semitones as tone duration 50 → 150 ms); segregation builds up over several seconds. **Strong.**

**A12. Below ~200 Hz the auditory filter is 25–50 Hz wide and temporal masking spans ~100–200 ms, so kick and sub-bass cannot be separated spectrally — only temporally.** Glasberg & Moore (1990) ERB formula; Jurado & Moore (2010) measured ERB 25–50 Hz for 31.5–100 Hz centres; 2022 forward-masking data show filters broader than the formula predicts below 1 kHz (Q_ERB ≈ 4 at 200 Hz). Forward masking decays over ~100–200 ms; loudness/threshold integration constant ~175–200 ms (Zwislocki). **Strong** on the numbers; **weak** on any direct study of kick-vs-sub perception (none found).

**A13. The lowest stream is the timing reference.** Hove et al. (2014): timing deviations detected better in the lower of two simultaneous streams; tapping follows the low stream; cochlear-model origin. Lenc et al. (2018): bass boosts beat-locked neural steady-state responses. Cameron et al. (2022): inaudible VLF raised concert movement 12%. **Strong.** This is why kick+bass "dominate": they own the timing pattern by physiology.

**A14. Timbre: blend and identifiability are inversely related; onsets carry the identity.** McAdams & Siedenburg (2019, citing Kendall & Carterette 1993); blend increases with slower attack and lower spectral centroid; Siedenburg et al. (2019) show the human onset advantage in instrument identification is explained by the acoustic richness of onsets. Krumhansl (2010): 400 ms "plinks" give artist + title >25% of the time. **Strong.**

**A15. Mixing-engineering masking metrics grounded in psychoacoustics exist.** Masked-to-Unmasked Ratio (Aichinger et al. 2011) = partial loudness in mix / loudness solo, on the Moore–Glasberg partial-loudness model; Hafezi & Reiss (2015) and Ronan et al. (2018) minimise it automatically; a 2021 mix-clarity predictor uses decomposition + masking thresholds. **Moderate** (AES/MIR validation, small listener panels). No perceptual study of sidechain ducking specifically was found.

## (d) Implications for a listener-model based on surprise and dwell time

**Measuring tonal/harmonic surprise from a note list.** Two layers, cheap to implement:

1. *Zero-order tonal surprise.* Sliding window (2–4 bars) pitch-class duration histogram → Pearson r against the 24 K–K profiles → key k* and key clarity MKC. Per note: tonal surprise ≈ −log₂( profile_k*[pc] / Σ profile_k* ). Key-clarity drift over time is itself a "new pattern emerging?" signal.
2. *Sequence surprise, IDyOM-style.* For each voice, viewpoints cpint (interval), cpintfref (scale degree relative to k*), and IOI-ratio. STM = PPM*/n-gram trained incrementally on the piece so far; LTM = corpus model if available, else omit or use the K–K profile as prior. p = entropy-weighted geometric mean of STM and LTM; IC = −log₂ p; H from the full distribution. For chords: alphabet = (scale degree, quality, inversion) relative to k*, as Cheung did. Report per-bar mean IC and mean H. Use *within-piece percentiles* (Cheung's upper/lower 40th) rather than absolute bits.

**Mapping to states.** "I have understood this pattern" = low IC *and* low H (STM has converged); "is a new pattern emerging?" = elevated IC with H falling across subsequent events (learning in progress). Boredom = dwell time in the first state beyond a threshold; chaos = sustained high IC with high H (no convergence). Pleasure is highest in transitions and in the two cross-quadrants; a dwell-time cost should grow in both corner states.

**Returning pattern: understood or distinct?** Distinct. Three reasons: (i) Cheung's "unsurprising event in an uncertain context" quadrant is exactly the reprise — the return is rewarded *because* the preceding section raised H; (ii) Huron/Salimpoor: reward accrues to correct prediction and to anticipation, so a predicted return pays even at zero surprise; (iii) Margulis: after a few hearings attention shifts to units of ~6 s, so the returning unit *is* the event being attended. Practically: an STM-only IC trace already contains the signature — a brief IC spike at the first event of the return collapsing to near-zero IC once the context matches. Detect "return" as: IC spike, then a run of minimum-IC events whose matched context length ≥ ~6 s of material, after an absence ≥ one section. Score reward ∝ H_before_return × match strength, decaying with the count of prior returns (Schellenberg: over-familiarity after ~8 focused exposures; don't penalise low IC per se — penalise *dwell*). Model absence itself as raising H (uncertainty about when/if the template returns), which is what makes return rewarding rather than boring.

**Measuring legible dominance of kick + bass (not loudness).** Dominance = the kick/bass pattern forms its own stream *and* that stream's pattern is stable. Below 200 Hz spectral carving is nearly unavailable (one ERB ≈ 25–50 Hz; a 50 Hz kick fundamental and a 45 Hz sub sit inside one filter), so legibility is almost entirely temporal, and A13 says the low stream carries the timing anyway.

From audio (mix + stems):
- *Onset survival.* Band-limit the mix to <150 Hz; detect onsets on the envelope; legibility_onset = fraction of kick-stem onsets that appear as distinct onsets in the mix's low band, where "distinct" = nearest bass onset ≥ 30 ms away (Rasch) *or* envelope dips by ≥ ~6 dB within the preceding 100–200 ms before the kick's rise.
- *Envelope decorrelation (common fate).* Pearson r between kick and bass envelopes in each ERB band below 200 Hz over a bar; r near +1 → fused single stream; r ≈ 0 or negative (sidechain ducking, alternating onsets) → two streams. Sidechain is, perceptually, the old-plus-new heuristic enforced by anti-correlated envelopes.
- *Pattern legibility.* Modulation spectrum of the low-band envelope; legibility_pattern = energy at the kick pattern's periodicities / total modulation energy (a Lenc-style beat-locked ratio). Being a ratio it is loudness-independent.
- *Spectral separation.* ΔERB = 21.4·log₁₀(1+4.37f_bass) − 21.4·log₁₀(1+4.37f_kick). If |ΔERB| < 1, require temporal separation; if the kick's audible identity lives in its click/harmonics (>150 Hz), measure MUR of the kick's upper band against the rest of the mix.

From note data (MIDI):
- Classify each bass onset relative to the nearest kick onset: |Δt| < 10–20 ms → fused; 30 ms ≤ |Δt| → segregated; bass note sustaining through a kick onset with the same pitch region → masked during the ~100 ms window unless the bass has a ≥ 30 ms envelope gap (ducking). Segregation score = fraction of kick onsets with a clear temporal window.
- Pitch: kick fundamental vs bass MIDI pitch in ERB units as above.
- Pattern stability: IC and H of the kick onset sequence and of the bass rhythm/pitch sequence from the STM. Dominance = segregation score × (1 − normalised H_kick). A bass whose onsets contradict the kick grid will, per Hove, steal the timing reference — count that as loss of dominance, whatever the levels.

One caveat: no perceptual study of kick-vs-sub separation or of sidechain ducking was found; the audio metrics above are constructed from the general masking/streaming numbers, not validated on that case.
