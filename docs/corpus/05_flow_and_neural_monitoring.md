# Flow, and monitoring the brain under music — research brief

> Corpus strand 5 of 5. Secondary research, 2026-09-08, sources verified by
> fetch; evidence graded. Written against the project's listening model:
> listening is travel between **P** ("pattern understood") and **Q** ("is a
> new pattern emerging?"), with disengagement when dwell time in either runs
> too long. "Surprise" = departure from what the current pattern predicts;
> "legible" = discernible regardless of loudness.

## (a) Core claims, with evidence quality

**1. Flow is defined by challenge–skill balance plus eight phenomenological features; it is a construct built for *doing*, not *listening*.** Csikszentmihalyi (1975) placed flow between boredom (skill > challenge) and anxiety (challenge > skill); Massimini & Carli (1988) expanded this to an eight-channel model (arousal, flow, control, boredom, relaxation, apathy, worry, anxiety). The Flow State Scale (Jackson & Marsh 1996; FSS-2, Jackson & Eklund 2002) measures nine dimensions with four items each. **Strong** as a self-report construct; the measurement instruments are validated mainly in sport and work. **Weak-to-moderate** as applied to passive listening: the "challenge" in listening is perceptual (tracking structure), which is exactly what the lead's model formalises but flow theory does not.

**2. Flow in listening is reported as *more* intense and more frequent than flow in performing.** Chirico et al. (2015) systematic review: listening produced flow more often than performance because performance anxiety and social appraisal interfere. Loepthien & Leipold (2022, N=207): more intense flow reported for listening than performing. Tan & Sin (2021) reviewed 1975–2019 flow-in-music literature: predominantly trait/dispositional, performance-heavy. **Moderate**, entirely self-report.

**3. Flow, absorption, trance and "the zone" are distinct but overlapping.** Absorption (Tellegen & Atkinson 1974) is a *trait* — openness to self-altering experience, linked to hypnotic susceptibility; the Absorption in Music Scale (Sandstrom & Russo 2013, 34 items) predicts strength of emotional response to music. Trance (Becker 2004; Herbert 2011) is a bodily event with strong emotion, loss of self, reduced inner language, often amnesia — closer to dissociation than to skilled control. Flow is a *state* that requires control and feedback. The distinction matters for the lead's model: absorption/trance are compatible with long dwell in P (repetitive, predictable input), whereas flow theory demands ongoing challenge, i.e. sustained Q. **Moderate** (mostly conceptual; the 2026 Aparicio-Terrés data below give the only direct evidence).

**4. Flow has a psychophysiological signature during music *performance*: higher HR, blood pressure, deeper respiration, more zygomaticus activity.** de Manzano et al. (2010): professional pianists, one piece played five times, flow rated after each; higher flow went with higher HR/BP (sympathetic), plus deeper breathing and smiling-muscle activity. **Moderate** (n small, within-subject, performance only — not transferable to listening, where motor load is absent).

**5. Neural correlates of flow: the fMRI picture is replicable; the EEG picture is not.**
- Ulrich et al. (2014, N=27 men; 2016, N=23; replication 2022, N=41 men), all mental arithmetic with adaptive difficulty in 3-min blocks: flow → relative *increase* in left inferior frontal gyrus and putamen (inverted-U across boredom/flow/overload), relative *decrease* in medial PFC and amygdala (U-shaped). The 2022 replication returned Bayes factors > 300 for both patterns and BF = 271 for electrodermal activity. **Strong for that task and population; men-only, arithmetic-only.**
- Dietrich (2004) "transient hypofrontality": flow requires suppression of explicit/prefrontal analytic systems. **Contested.** Ulrich's data show *increased* lateral PFC (IFG, DLPFC) with *decreased* medial PFC — a selective, not global, hypofrontality. Harris, Vine & Wilson (2017) reframe flow as an attentional-network interaction with dopaminergic/noradrenergic modulation; van der Linden et al. (2021) push a locus coeruleus–norepinephrine account. Alameda et al. (2022) systematic review of 25 studies (471 participants total, samples 6–41): "sparse and inconclusive."
- Katahira et al. (2018, N=16, 64-ch EEG, arithmetic): frontal theta higher in flow *and* overload vs boredom; frontocentral alpha graded with difficulty. Sugino et al. (bioRxiv 2024, 7 participants, 920 gameplay trials, within-subject): **no** FMT–flow correlation in 6 of 7 participants; one positive, one negative. Their diagnosis: prior work confounded task difficulty with flow, used unfamiliar tasks, too-short trials, and group averaging. **Weak / contested.** The "frontal-midline theta = flow" claim should not be used.
- Default-mode suppression during flow: supported indirectly by mPFC/PCC decreases in Ulrich; a 2025 Frontiers piece argues for *enhanced* DMN–executive coupling instead. **Weak.**

**6. Musical reward is a dopaminergic anticipation–resolution loop, and pleasure peaks at intermediate predictability.** Blood & Zatorre (2001, PET): chills scale with ventral striatum, midbrain, amygdala, OFC blood flow. Menon & Levitin (2005, fMRI): NAcc–VTA coupling during listening. Salimpoor et al. (2011, PET raclopride): dopamine release in caudate during *anticipation*, NAcc at *peak*. Salimpoor et al. (2013, Science): NAcc activity and NAcc–auditory-cortex connectivity predicted money bid for unheard music. Gold et al. (2019): inverted-U between predictability and liking; a listener-specific "sweet spot for surprise" weighted by context uncertainty. Cheung et al. (2019, ~80,000 chords): pleasure highest for *low-uncertainty context + high-surprise chord* or *high-uncertainty context + low-surprise chord*, with amygdala, hippocampus and auditory cortex tracking the interaction. **Strong** (converging PET, fMRI, behavioural, computational).

**7. Expectation violation has fast, well-characterised electrophysiological markers.** ERAN (Koelsch 2000/2002): negativity 150–250 ms after a syntactically unexpected chord, pre-attentive, larger in musicians. Rhythmic MMNm/P3am (Vuust et al.): ~110–130 ms and ~+80 ms after a syncopation, magnitude weighted by rhythmic complexity ("predictive coding of rhythmic incongruity"). **Strong** for existence; **moderate** for reading them in single trials or with consumer gear.

**8. Cortical activity phase-locks to beat and meter, is amplified by bass, and correlates with movement and beat perception.** Nozaradan et al. (2011, 2012): steady-state evoked potentials at beat (e.g. 2.4 Hz) and imagined meter frequencies, enhanced relative to acoustic energy. Doelling & Poeppel (2015, MEG): delta–theta (<8 Hz) tracking across tempi; no tracking below ~1 note/s in non-musicians. Tal et al. (2017): 2 Hz neural response present even when the acoustics contain no 2 Hz energy (syncopated "missing pulse"); larger 2 Hz amplitude → earlier tapping. Lenc et al. (2018): beat-frequency locking selectively enhanced with 130 Hz vs 1237 Hz tones. Cameron et al. (2022, live Orphx EDM concert, motion-capture): +12% movement when sub-threshold very-low-frequency speakers were on. **Strong** for the phenomenon; **contested** as to mechanism — Novembre & Iannetti (2018) and Rajendran & Schnupp (2019) argue frequency-tagging can't distinguish oscillatory entrainment from a train of evoked responses. For a listener model this distinction is immaterial: the signal indexes beat *legibility* either way.

**9. Inter-subject correlation (ISC) of EEG is the best-validated neural proxy for *engagement* with music.** Madsen et al. (2019, N=40): ISC drops with repetition of familiar-style music, stays stable for unfamiliar style; attention manipulation confirms ISC tracks engagement. Kaneshiro et al. (2020, N=48; 2024, N=23, 128-ch): ISC in 5-s windows rises during tension *before* climaxes and *falls at the highpoint itself*; intact music beats envelope-matched noise. Rai et al. (2025, iScience): delta-band (1–4 Hz) audience synchrony, up to 23 mobile-EEG headsets, higher live than in lab, β = 0.03 for high vs low engagement. **Moderate–strong**, but ISC is a group measure: it needs many listeners of the *same* track.

**10. Peripheral physiology synchronises across concert audiences and tracks arousal and engagement.** Czepiel et al. (2021, N=98): 10-s sliding-window ISC of HR, respiration, SCR; faster tempo → more synchrony and higher self-reported engagement (r = 0.48). Tschacher et al. (2023, N=132): HR synchrony correlated with emotional engagement and immersion; 53.7% HR data lost. Chabin et al. (2022): 12 Emotiv headsets in a concert audience, theta (4–8 Hz) inter-brain coherence higher when shared pleasure was high and people sat close. Solberg & Dibben (2019, N=24, EDM): SCR significantly higher at the *drop* than at breakdown or build-up. **Moderate**; correlational and stimulus-specific.

**11. Trance/ASC from rhythmic sound: trait absorption dominates, entrainment adds little.** Aparicio-Terrés et al. (2026, N=27): 2 Hz drumming for 12 min raised "unity" vs scrambled drums (p = 0.049); Tellegen absorption predicted disembodiment and spiritual experience regardless of rhythmicity; **neural entrainment (2 Hz SNR) did not predict phenomenology**. Gordon et al. (2026, N=40): 10-min drumming at 1.5/4/9.5 bps; theta-rate phase tracking correlated with altered-experience scores (rho = 0.39). Fachner et al. (2019): single-dyad GIM hyperscanning, frontal alpha asymmetry cross-correlation ~0.14–0.18. **Weak–moderate.**

**12. Contested/weak: neurofeedback, "brainwave music", alpha/theta over-reading.** Thibault & Raz (2017): EEG neurofeedback benefits track placebo; sham feedback performs as well. Binaural-beat entrainment: García-Argibay et al. (2019) meta-analysis g = 0.45, but a 2023 PLOS One systematic review found 5/14 studies supporting and 8 contradicting oscillatory entrainment. Frontal alpha asymmetry failed a 270-analysis multiverse replication for depression (eLife 2021). Alpha is an active-inhibition/gating signal (Klimesch 2007; Jensen & Mazaheri 2010), not "relaxation." **Contested; do not build on these.**

**Not found:** any peer-reviewed physiological or EEG study of DJs, or DJ–crowd hyperscanning. The nearest are Rudnicki et al. (2026) — behavioural coding of DJ behaviour in Belgian clubs vs crowd affect, no physiology — and the Cambridge Companion to EDM chapter on dance-floor flow (ethnographic).

## (d) Implications for a listener-model based on surprise and dwell time

**Where flow sits in the two-state model.** The evidence does not support "flow = long dwell in P." Everything measurable that tracks engagement — NAcc/caudate dopamine, Gold's and Cheung's inverted-U, Witek's syncopation inverted-U, Kaneshiro's ISC rising *during tension* and falling *at* the climax, Solberg's SCR peaking at the drop — rewards the *transition*, not either endpoint. Flow, as Csikszentmihalyi defined it, is the condition in which challenge is continuously re-matched to skill; in the lead's vocabulary that is a **steady, sustainable alternation**: Q resolving into P at a rate the listener can keep up with, then a fresh Q before P goes stale. Absorption/trance is the different case — repetitive, low-surprise input, long P-dwell, driven by trait absorption more than by the stimulus (Aparicio-Terrés). So the model should carry two engagement modes, not one: **flow-like alternation** (surprise budget spent regularly) and **trance-like dwell** (surprise budget near zero, held by beat legibility and bass). Both are reachable; only the first depends on the composer's control of pattern.

**Two things the literature adds to the lead's model.** First, Cheung's interaction: the same surprise is worth more in a low-uncertainty context. Legibility (P firmly established) *earns* the right to a bigger surprise; in an ambiguous context, a *smaller* surprise is the pleasurable one. Dwell time in P is therefore not waste — it is the setup that makes the next Q legible. Second, Kaneshiro's ISC drops at the highpoint: the moment of resolution is where listeners *diverge* (each resolves into private response), so the best neural signal of "still travelling" is elevated ISC/entrainment in the run-up, not at the payoff.

**What signal most credibly indicates "still travelling" vs "disengaged."**
- Best single-listener neural candidate: **beat-rate phase-locking / SS-EP amplitude** (delta-theta, fronto-central). It indexes beat legibility, is amplified by bass, and predicts movement. Loss of it means the pattern has stopped being discernible or the listener has stopped tracking. Caveat: it does not distinguish P from Q; it says the pattern is being followed.
- Best marker of Q→P transition: **an SCR 1–3 s after a structural event** (drop, voice entry, harmonic turn). Absence of SCRs across several intended surprises is the clearest disengagement flag available with consumer hardware.
- Best marker of motivated travel on a slower timescale: **HRV.** Both the arithmetic-flow and Tetris-flow work found HRV lowest and HR moderately raised in flow relative to boredom; boredom looks like a resting profile. A listener whose RMSSD drifts up toward baseline while HR settles is probably no longer travelling.
- Do not use: frontal-midline theta, frontal alpha asymmetry, any "alpha = relaxed engagement" reading, and any single-trial ERAN/MMN from a 4-channel headband.

**An honest one-person calibration setup.**
1. Hardware: Polar H10 chest strap (RMSSD interchangeable with ECG); an EDA sensor on the fingers or a wrist EDA device kept *still* — E4-class wrist HRV collapses to r ≈ 0 with movement, so seated listening only; optional Muse or 8-ch OpenBCI for beat SS-EP (Fz/Cz-region signal; TP9/TP10 on Muse is temporal, marginal for this). Motion sensor (phone IMU) as the cheap proxy for "wanting to move."
2. Stimuli: own tracks plus two controls — a phase-scrambled or measure-shuffled version (Kaneshiro's design) to remove pattern while keeping spectrum, and a loop of a single bar held for 2–3 min to induce deliberate over-dwell in P.
3. Windows: 10-s sliding windows for HR/RR/SCR-event counts (Czepiel), 30–120 s for RMSSD, ≥30 s of steady tempo per SS-EP estimate; each track heard at least 5× on separate days (Madsen: repetition reduces engagement for familiar material, so the *decline curve* is itself a dwell-time measurement).
4. Ground truth: continuous slider ("am I still listening?") plus a post-hoc Flow Short Scale and AIMS once, to know the trait baseline.
5. Analysis: within-person only (Sugino's lesson); time-lock SCRs to the marked structural events; compare SS-EP amplitude in legible vs deliberately blurred sections; treat any correlation below the person's own noise floor over five sessions as absent.
6. Expectations: SCR-to-drop and beat-locking are the two signals likely to survive a single-subject consumer setup. HRV will separate boredom from engagement at the minute scale but not resolve individual pattern transitions. EEG ISC and dopamine measures are out of reach for one listener. Anything claiming per-bar "flow" from a forehead headband is marketing.
